Akela couldn’t remember where he was. He couldn’t remember *why* he was here. He was… in Sev’reon? Of course he was. He lived here, didn’t he? Or did he live somewhere else? He didn’t know what he was remembering.

Someone slammed into him from behind, sending them both sprawling to the ground. The stranger, a woman, scrambled to her feet and continued running without even addressing Akela. He found himself looking up from under a sea of people, all running, fleeing from something. Far up beyond them, wider than the span of his narrowing vision, was Sev’reon’s globe of pure manifested Aura. The city’s defensive armament was normally comforting, but now the barrier was teetering on a knife’s edge and threatening to fail. Instead of the smooth spherical ethereal surface he expected, he instead saw a much larger dome of flickering geometry and hexagonal facets. Entire regions of segments were missing, and thin, weak panels blinked in and out of existence as they were needed to protect the capital.

From deep within him, as if he were a stranger in his own body, something pushed him to stand. His body dragged itself to its feet, and the current of people mercifully parted before him. The thunder and noise of disaster slowly filtered in through his damaged senses. This was not the Sev’reon he knew, it couldn’t be.

This Sev’reon was dying.

He kneeled down to touch the ground, and as he did a pillar of water and then ice formed beneath him, lifting him up above the chaos. Patches of the hexagonal dome continued to dim and flicker as they struggled to manifest under reduced power. A dark mass of… *something* crashed against the barrier in the distance, causing the throng of civilians to rebound and scatter like ants in a new direction. Akela felt a thought settle itself gently in his consciousness. A calm, horrifying thought. Just simply, *“I am witnessing the end of the world.”*

The thought resonated through his empty lonely mind, but then a crack of Thunder snapped him back into control of his body. A series of lighting columns burrowed straight down through the dark pile of shadowy limbs crawling at the barrier, as a streaking shape flew over the swarm on crackling wings. Then another dark shape made a pass of its own. A dragon this time, spouting intertwined jets of orange and teal flames down at the inky horrors. The tide of darkness retreated, shrinking under the haze of fire and plasma.

Akela looked up to the floating peak of Sev’reon’s mountain. Some of its enormous chain tethers were missing, broken and haphazardly coiled in the rubble of buildings below. The peak hung uncomfortably in space, canted at an angle, threatening to come loose and fall up and away. At least the mountain’s heart, the city’s Aura reservoir, still glowed and pumped its energy down through the remaining conduits to the ground. Another thought, this time a question: *“What could humanity have possibly done to deserve this…”*

And then, the screaming masses slowly quieted. People stopped moving. They stood still, turned, pointed, held each other, closed their eyes, and refused to believe. High up in the atmosphere, far, far above the fragile dome, the dark clouds parted. The smoke and mist were pushed down and away, as what Akela’s mind could only tell him was a Diety fell from the veiled stratosphere. An ancient serpent of infinite wings and eyes, bloodied and broken, fell down into our mortal world. From even higher still, glowing with malice, another impossible being with 8 burning eyes looked down on everyone with a hateful joyous reptilian smile. Its claws pierced the sky first, and then its palm pushed several square miles of clouds down toward the city. The size and distance of the thing made the attack appear unbearably slow. Slow enough that you had to wait to die, but fast enough that you’d never outrun its width before death arrived.

As the end of all things bore down on the world, two small lights rose up above the buildings on the far side of the mountain. The wind picked up and began to slowly spiral around them. Akela felt something magical fluctuate nearby. It was something of his, in his pocket. He slipped his hand in to retrieve it, but the second his skin touched its etched metallic surface he remembered.

“Aster.”

The two lights. They aren’t just lights in the distance, they are people. The green one is Aster. He knows Aster, he knows him from the academy. What is he doing? What can any of them hope to accomplish?

Before he could finish his thoughts, his heart took over. Akela lept from his frozen pillar and, with magic he did not understand, he took flight. He latched on to the only part of this dying world that he remembered seemed real, and resolved to help his friend, somehow. He flew over streets and buildings, and as he did so the black dragon and its rider fell into formation alongside him. He paid them no attention.

Then, in his own mind, he heard Aster speak.

“I need Sev to disable the barrier. I don’t know who I can reach like this anymore. If you can hear me, shut down the whole city and send me every bit of power the heart can manage, just for a minute.”

The dragon to Akela’s right instantly rolled and, with a beat of its scarred wings, thundered off toward the floating peak. Whoever they were, they must have been able to hear Aster as well. It was a small comfort to Akela that they seemed to know what they were doing. It would be nice to remember what that felt like.

“Be ready to put the barrier back up when I’m gone. I just don’t want to damage what we’ve got left when we do this.”

When he’s gone? What is he talking about?

Moments later, the hum of the city collapsed and the shield dome faded into nothing. The silence was eerie, and the sounds of crumbling stone, fires crackling, and the feet of hundreds of people shuffling suddenly stuck out in stark relief. The low, frightened murmuring was again audible as people tried to make peace with their end.

Akela could see Aster’s silhouette now. His aura was burning brighter than he had ever seen. It wasn’t even green anymore. It was white, and he was haloed from behind with that impossible intense light as he levitated in open space alongside his stormy friend. He held up a hand and pointed at the hovering mountain’s heart, calling down an intense beam of raw Aura that he captured in his own Dynamic’s refraction.

Akela did not understand what had happened to the capital. He did not understand how his friend had become so strong. The machinery and technology of the city was foreign to him. He did not understand how energy could be projected like this, or what Aster was actually doing, but he could feel the horror and amazement welling up in his gut over what it would no doubt cost him.

Aster channeled the crystal’s raw Aura into functional magic, projecting it forward into the space before him. The intensity of the beam multiplied. The mountain’s heart brightened as its output increased, and the floating mountain peak’s slow unnoticeable movements became agitated as it strained against it's remaining tethers. Aster put his other hand out towards his hovering friend. It was hard for Akela to make out details through the storm and the slow arcing strands of lighting that emanated from her, but he could see the unmistakable shape of large golden horns curling and branching back from her temples.

A second beam then connected Aster and his friend, before she began to accelerate. Her wreath of wind and lighting circled her with rising fury, forming first into a vortex, then flattening into a crisp cutting disk of pressure and light. He channeled more and more Aura from the reservoir, through himself, and ultimately over to his wrathful-looking friend. Akela pieced together what he was doing. The effects of time acceleration were not quite obvious, but he was likely accelerating his friend through time. He was trying to squeeze as much energy as possible out of the mountain’s heart and into her without burning her out.

Akela knew there was no way Aster was going to survive this. Accelerating himself like he was doing for the other could not protect him from the effects of channeling so much power. It would only increase the load on him even further. There was no clever trick or exploit this time. Already, the damage was evident. The dark haze of magical byproducts emanated from him like the negative corona of a dark star. Crystal circuitry tattooed into his skin burned itself into light, and then nothing, strand by strand. Even if Akela could reach him before he burned out every biological and artificial fiber of his body, he didn’t know what could be done. Aura Poisoning was one of the few things you can’t necessarily just use more magic to fix.

Akela, and probably at least a few others, then heard Aster speak to them in their minds one more time. Just a single, short, somber phrase. Aster was never particularly flashy with his magic if he didn’t have to be. He didn’t name his spells or announce his moves. But this time was different. Maybe he just wanted to see what it was like, to see if he had been missing out. Maybe he thought there was a chance it might actually help, and he decided to pull out every stop, no matter how minor or silly.

“... Moment of Eternity: ... Lorentz Cascade…”

Pink and white lightning arced straight out from the horned mage in all directions, evaporating the air around her beyond plasma and into pure energy. Akela was already deaf before his brain even registered the shockwave hitting him. A cylinder of lighting, the width of multiple city blocks, simply *appeared* out from her hands fully formed, aimed upwards towards the ancient annihilator’s descending palm. It was no longer individual bolts of electricity she manifested. The time acceleration and the sheer quantity of channeled Aura was so great that it was just a solid conduit of raw power.

It phased straight through the palm of the world-ender and continued tearing up through the rest of its limb. Akela did not know if Greater Spirits had anatomy like people did, but it was clear that this was a grievous injury, even for an immortal. Most of its palm was vaporized. Some fingers were separated, and what pieces were left of those digits continued crashing to the ground. The wounded Deity recoiled slowly in horror and pain, averting a fraction of certain disaster from the city, for at least a few more minutes.

The display of human spite and resourcefulness did not last long though. After a few moments of normal time, the beams both stopped, Aster’s aura went dark, and he fell to earth along with the celestial carnage.

As he fell, the shield dome flickered back into existence. The city would have audibly hummed back to life as power was restored if Akela still had eardrums to hear it. His world was gone. This was all wrong. It wasn’t fair. He didn’t understand why he was being made to lose yet another friend.

He silently screamed and reached out toward Aster’s weightless blackened body. Wings and talons sprouted from Akela’s body, only to forcibly wrap himself up in a cocoon of water, threatening to drown him inside himself. He swam and thrashed and rejected everything. He denied the world he had seen. He rejected his fear even as he felt himself dragged down deeper into the dark of the ocean of his mind. The only thing he allowed himself to feel was anger, and blame. Blame directed at anything even remotely tangible. Blame especially directed at the cold glowing blue eyes that judged him here, this far under the waves.

The eyes observed him for a while, before eventually Akela was ejected from the being’s domain, and as he re-entered his new horrid reality he fell roughly into a stranger’s arms. The two of them were still falling, but slowly, lofted by summoning magic. That familiar black dragon dove down alongside them and gently caught them on its back, as it angled its wings and pulled away from the ground. The stranger held him firmly as Akela came back to most of his senses. The man’s skin and clothing burned perpetually with those same teal flames he had seen push back the darkness. Intense, blasting flames poured off of him from head to toe, with the exception of his left eye. His eye was dark, blood ran from it down his face and neck, and a secondary invisible flame burned from his injury. Its missing mantle carved out negative space from the rest of his wreath of flames.

*“This was their **resting** Aura?”* Akela thought to himself. What age of war and struggle had he found himself in that produced so many battlemages of this caliber?

The stranger shook Akela and spoke at him. His lips moved, but all sound had abandoned Akela, along with hope and will. The stranger spoke again and this time he pointed as the dragon flew back towards the barrier. Akela looked, and saw hexagons fading one by one, lower and lower down a section of the shield wall as a new wave of dark homunculi crashed against it. He looked back to the stranger, and this time he could feel the meaning behind the words on his lips.

Akela could hear a harsh and familiar phrase repeat in his mind, *“You are being tested.”*

He resolved his expression into one of focus and nodded at his new friend.

Having said all he needed, the stranger leapt from the back of his dragon, flared his Aura, and etched a burning path through the sky toward the world-ender. His afterimage crashed through an already fractured and failing shield panel high up on the globe as he punched a hole through space.

The dragon pulled hard, looping up and banking over, then back down, bringing them both toward the failing section of the barrier at ground level. Akela closed his eyes and repeated to himself what he could not stop thinking: *“You are being tested.”* After a moment of meditation, he opened his eyes, stood up against the wind ripping over the dragon’s beating wings, and said,

*“I have never failed a test before. This will be no different.”*

Akela couldn’t remember where he was. He couldn’t remember *when* he was.

His eyes blasted open. Free from his vision, he thrashed and tore at something holding him down. He was enveloped. Drowning. A figure loomed over him in the dark with grasping reaching talons. He kicked at his cocoon, freeing an arm, and with it he pushed the hands away. He ripped the blanket from his body and lurched to the edge of his bed, sick to his stomach, every nerve buzzing with adrenalin.

He nearly fell against the door, desperate to escape, barely aware of the stranger speaking to him, following him. Out of the room, down the hall, forward and onward and away and out, onto the summer grass of the campus courtyard. Only when he felt the fresh air on his skin, calm and gentle, did he feel like he could breath.

He was… in Sev’reon? Of course he was. He lived here, didn’t he? Or did he live somewhere else? He didn’t know what he was remembering.

He found himself looking up from under a sea of stars, all shining, apart from everything. Closer down to the earth, and wider than the span of his narrowing vision, was Sev’reon’s globe of pure manifested Aura. The city’s defensive armament was comforting. Instead of the larger dome of flickering geometry and hexagonal facets he feared to see, he instead saw the smooth spherical ethereal surface he expected. The entire dome was as it should be. Whole and stoic, standing by to protect the capital.

This Sev’reon was sleeping. Alive, but sleeping.

The hands of his roommate slowly slid up over his shoulders from behind, pulling him into a hug. Akela lifted his own hands up to press Theo's arms against his chest, and golden strands filtered past his vision as Theo dipped his head. Akela could only cry as Theo supported him, holding him in the midnight airglow as the stars fell overhead.

"It's ok, I'm here, Akela. I'm not going anywhere..."

Akela felt his tears falling onto his hands as he clung to the world. They were hot, and heavy. He opened his eyes and went to wipe his nose, but when he was met instead with a shock of pain, he pulled back. Blood coated his hand, not tears. He touched his face again to find blood dripping from his nose, down into the snow beneath his feat.

Snow?

His head shot up, panic rising in him all over again. Before him stood Kaito, sheathed obsidian blade in hand, scowling. Blood dripping from his fist as well.

"We'll be together forever, I promise..."

Akela's knees crumpled out from beneath him as he barely choked out a strangled "No..."

A searing blade of agony cut through him from shoulder to waist. Kaito cradled him.

"I need to know you'll be able to protect yourself when I'm not here..."

Akela's limb body slipped from Kaito's grasp and hit the cold stone floor. His head lolled to the side, and in the flickering fire of his burning home he was forced to watch as a masked assassin ripped a blade from his father's chest. From just out of view, a shadowplay of violence was projected from the flames onto the wall behind his lifeless father. His mother was cut down next. Not quickly. She fought, and killed, but not enough. She too fell.

"We just want what's best for you..."

From deep within him, as if he were a stranger in his own body, something pushed him to rise. Barely living, his body dragged itself to its knees, and one of the assassins above him plunged a blade into his body. The smoke and heat of disaster slowly faded out through his damaged senses. This was not the life he knew, it couldn’t be. He could not bear to remember again.

"You are nothing but a vessel..."

Then nothing. Nothing for ages. Nothing for as long as Tanele could remember. Trapped in the nothingness between living. Waiting. Waiting.

Akela couldn’t remember where he was. He couldn’t remember *who* he was.

His eyes blast open. Free from his vision, he rips the cold sweat from his body into a sheet of floating pointed needle shards. He wields his improvised weaponry against the empty walls of his tent, only to find that he is alone in the dark. He scrambles for his glowstone, breathing out the command word and lighting it up in his clenched fist. He rips himself from his bedroll and lurches to the door of his tent, sick to his stomach, every nerve buzzing with adrenalin.

The tiny icicles clink softly to the ground as he releases his magic, and he fights his way forward and onward and away and out, into the damp swamp air of the swalscape. Only when he feels the fresh air on his skin, calm and gentle, does he feel like he can breath.

He was… in Sev’reon? Of course he is. He lives here, but he doesn't want to. Or had he already died long ago? He doesn't want to have to keep asking.

He finds himself looking up from under a canopy of trees, all rustling, connected to everything. Sheltered beneath the leaves, and distant in the vertigo of his narrowing vision, are the tents of his closest companions. The camp's quiet stillness is comforting. Instead of the fear and helplessness of the cycle he was stuck in, he instead looks for hope and support in his friends. The world is as it should be.

This Sev’reon is a lie. It always was.

Akela blinks hot and heavy tears back from his eyes as he clutches himself in the darkness of forest. He cranes his head back, looking up past the canopy with eyes shut tight, waiting for the dream to take him back. Minutes pass, and when he dares to hope that he is well and truly free, as free as he ever is, he deflates and turns to reenter his tent.

As he crouches down to crawl back through the opening, out of the corner of his eye he spies another light. Something across the way in Aster's tent glows softly, on and off, once or twice. The light from within Aster's tent fades out fully, and Akela waits to see if he stirs. Aster seemingly does not. Perhaps it was a notification of some kind from his interface?

Akela slowly pulls open the flap to his tent without entering. He waits there, thinking in the dark. Thinking about everything, and nothing.

"You know you can always trust me..."

Akela drops his head and mutters, "Damn it."

He leans in, and collects his bedroll and pillow, closes the tent flap behind him, and timidly approaches Aster's tent. With a deep breath and a shaking heart, he taps at the taut fabric.

Something inside glows once again.

This time it doesn't go out.
