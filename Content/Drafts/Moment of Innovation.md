“…~Pwincess~?”

At this, she smirked. “You’re funny. I’m always happy to see a fellow [magic school] graduate, but I don’t recall burdening you with that particular decree.”

Nervously, Aster clarified, “I’m not technically a graduate, either.”

Her grin flattened and she said, “I’ll be completely honest with you, that’s not a strong opening.”

“If there had been a full degree available for my chosen specialization, then I’d have taken it.”

She sat back in her ornate chair, rhythmically tapping her fingers as she considered his excuse. “Interesting.”

All Aster could do was sit and wait while she thought, and hope that she wouldn’t just dismiss him.

“Have you brought me a resume?”

“Oh, yes, of course.” He said, and as he spoke he summoned a tiny mana reserve into his hand.

Aster held out his hand, offering the small emerald chip of crystal. His posture was passive. He presented the gem plainly in his open palm, unpossessively, waiting for someone to take it from him. Sev’erna’s guard quickly moved to take it, but they were swiftly shut down with a snap of the Princess’s fingers and a glare. As she walked out from around her desk her aura emerged gently, and with a wave of her hand she projected a pulse of magic into the room ahead of her. Aster felt his own aura briefly displaced along the astral dimension and into the visible world as it pushed through him. The same was true of the royal guard beside him, the gem in his hand, and any other magical objects in the spell’s wake. Any latent or residual aura was momentarily made visible to the naked eye, and both Aster and his gem appeared as though they were backlit with a bright green light.

Seemingly satisfied that the small crystal reserve was as Aster had said, she took it from him. She looked at it with a passive curiousness, and then turned her eyes back down towards him.

“I’m sorry, I have a written version as well, I just though-“

She cut him off before he could summon his second copy. “No, you were right, this is better.”

The gem glowed as she mentally accessed its contents. Again, all Aster could do was wait for the Princess to readdress him.

“Computational Augmentation.” She said, as she scryed through the reserve’s contents. “I’ll admit, I’m not very familiar with that field of study.”

“It’s a relatively young discipline, but one that I believe will be the future of magic.”

The light from the gem faded and the Princess leaned back against the front of her desk, still looking down at Aster. “Explain it to me. Convince me of this future you see.”
